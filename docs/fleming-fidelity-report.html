Fleming's model, as the sources give it

These are the load-bearing statements the simulations should honour. Each is tied to where Fleming says it.

Space is a sea of zeptonsShort-lived, massless particle–antiparticle pairs that are true electric dipoles (an electron-like end and a positron-like end). They exist over a continuum of energies.
Book ch. 4–5; Photon paper §3
Each zepton is a Planck oscillatorEnergy E = hf/2, and it can last no longer than ΔE·Δt = h/2, so its lifetime is one period, 1/f.
Book ch. 2 (Eq. 2-4), ch. 4; Casimir paper §8
A zepton's life has a shapeThe pair is produced at a point, separates to the full extent of its wavelength, then collapses back and annihilates. Its wavelength is as far as it can reach in its lifetime: λ = c/f.
Electron paper §8; Book ch. 6 (p. 55), ch. 19 (Fig. 19-4)
A zepton can turn 180° at mostHalf a turn in a lifetime is the limit, and reaching it is what the speed of light is. Fields need only a tiny fraction of that.
Electron paper §6; Book ch. 4, ch. 19
The electric field is zeptons alignedPositive ends face a negative charge. Only a small share of zeptons turn, and only slightly. The same total polarization crosses every sphere around the charge, spread ever thinner.
Book ch. 6 (Figs 6-2, 6-4, 6-7); Electron paper §2
Charge belongs to the fieldA particle is a bare point polarizer. Unit charge is the same for every particle because the polarizability of the field is the same everywhere.
Electron paper §2; Fine-structure paper §3
The magnetic field is zeptons rotatingThey turn to stay aligned with a moving charge, on opposite sides in opposite senses. The field direction is the rotation axis (right-hand grip). Replacements carry the rotation on.
Book ch. 6 (Figs 6-14 to 6-16)
Spin is rotation during polarizationNew zeptons are born at random angles and turn toward alignment. Neighbours turning the same way cost least energy, so there is one preferred sense. Nothing orbits; dipole centres stay put.
Electron paper §3 (Fig. 2), §6; Radii paper §1
g ≈ 2 from both chargesA rotating dipole carries a positive end inside and a negative end outside, moving oppositely: two counter-rotating shells of opposite charge.
Electron paper §4; Radii paper §1
Mass is excluded zero-point energyTreat the particle as a shell that excludes zeptons with wavelengths between its inner and outer diameters. The energy excluded equals mc²; for the electron the shell sits at the Compton wavelength.
Book ch. 16 (Eq. 16-1); Electron paper §5
A photon is a chain of induced zeptonsOne central pair per half-wavelength, each rotating 180°, each counter-rotating to the last. The pair carries half the energy; the surrounding field of slightly biased zeptons carries the rest.
Photon paper §2, §5 (Fig. 3); Book ch. 5 (Figs 5-1, 5-2)
A photon's field lives and dies with each pairThe field is extinguished every half-wavelength with the central zepton, then reverses polarity and induces the next pair.
Book ch. 5 (p. 44), ch. 19 (p. 146)
Two speedsPairs and matter are limited to c (a full 180° turn). Fields spread dipole to dipole far faster, because each dipole turns only a sliver.
Photon paper §6; Book ch. 19
All force is pushZeptons press on matter from every side. Motion comes from a pressure difference, as between Casimir plates where long wavelengths are shut out.
Book ch. 3 (Figs 3-3, 3-4); Casimir paper §2
Attraction: the chain shortensBetween opposite charges the aligned zeptons collapse, neighbours fill in, and each replacement has less room, so pressure between the charges drops.
Casimir paper §4 (Fig. 3); Book ch. 6 (Fig. 6-12)
Repulsion: the gap fillsBetween like charges the aligned zeptons meet like-end to like-end, deflect, and open gaps. New randomly oriented zeptons fill them and push in all directions.
Casimir paper §4 (Fig. 4); Book ch. 6 (Figs 6-9, 6-10)

Findings and fixes

Each card says what Fleming states, what the laboratory did, and what changes. Cards turn green as fixes land.

Medium

M1 Zeptons spun a full turn; Fleming allows half done

Fleming
A zepton can rotate 180 degrees during its existence; a half-wavelength of light is a virtual electron-positron pair that rotates 180 degrees; they cannot rotate farther or faster than that.
Laboratory before
Every pair rotated 2π·f·age: one complete 360° turn per lifetime, twice the limit that Fleming identifies with the speed of light.

Fixed. Each pair turns through a half-turn over its life (phase = phase₀ + π·f·age). The model is now medium-lifecycle/3; version 1 and 2 files open and are upgraded with a note in the event log. A new test checks the turn is monotonic and never exceeds 180°.

Electron paper §6 (p. 5); Book ch. 4 (p. 39), ch. 19 (pp. 147–148)

M2 Every zepton opened to the same width, whatever its energy done

Fleming
The pair separates to the full extent of their wavelength, and the maximum distance a zepton can occupy in space during the time it exists is equal to its wavelength, λ = c/f. Fig. 3-3 draws the fluctuations as ovals of many sizes.
Laboratory before
One slider set a single peak separation for all pairs. A pair at three times the frequency lived a third as long but opened just as wide.

Fixed. Peak separation scales with each pair's own wavelength (∝ 1/f, the same factor as its lifetime). The slider is now the peak for a pair at the reference frequency f₀. The true scale cannot be drawn (at f₀ the wavelength c/f₀ is about 30 L₀ and the cell is 8 L₀), so the proportion is kept and the size is reduced; the inspector shows each pair's real c/f beside its drawn separation.

Clarity. A faint line now joins the two lobes of each pair. Without it, widely opened pairs read as a scatter of unrelated dots. The Slow oscillations preset was retuned so its long-wavelength pairs stay inside the cell.

Electron paper §8 (p. 5); Book ch. 3 (Fig. 3-3), ch. 19 (Fig. 19-4)

Light

L1 The central pair was a fixed size and lay along the path when widest done

Fleming
The dipole wavelength is equal to the distance traveled by a photon during a half-wavelength. Figs 4-9, 5-1 and Fig. 3 of the photon paper draw each pair across the path, with the electric field along the pair and one polarity per pair.
Laboratory before
Peak separation was a fixed 0.42 L at every wavelength. At the default phase the pair was transverse only at birth and death, when it has no width, and lay along the direction of travel at mid-life.

Fixed. The pair opens to λ/2, is born along the path, and lies along the polarization axis at mid-life, alternating polarity pair by pair as in Fleming's figures. Its lobes are drawn larger than the background and joined by a line. Pair start angle chooses where in this half-turn the run begins (at 90° the first pair is across the path, fully open, at tick 0) and never changes a pair's geometry. Model is now light-induction/2; older files open unchanged.

Photon paper §2 (Fig. 3); Book ch. 4 (Fig. 4-9), ch. 5 (Fig. 5-1)

L2 The field was a frozen wave-packet gliding along, unconnected to the pairs done

Fleming
The rotating pair produces the field. The fields are also extinguished each half wavelength along with the central zepton. When that pair is annihilated, the surrounding field reverses electric and magnetic polarity … Those fields induce a new central particle pair. The field radiates outward from the central zepton and declines … [as] the inverse of the distance squared.
Laboratory before
A prescribed one-cycle envelope slid along at c with a fixed shape. At the moving centre the field never changed, whatever the pair was doing.

Fixed. Field strength and polarity are now the transverse part of the active pair's dipole moment. The field swells and collapses with the pair, is zero at each hand-off, reverses for the next pair, and falls with the square of distance from that pair in every direction. The magnetic field lies along the pair's rotation axis (right-hand grip) and electric × magnetic points along the travel direction; tests check both at every pair start angle, direction and polarization. The field-reach cage shrinks to nothing at each hand-off, with the pair. Surrounding pairs are now randomly oriented and lean toward the active pair only where its field reaches them, as in Fig. 5-2. A fixed probe sees alternating pulses, one per pair, at the photon's frequency.

Book ch. 5 (pp. 43–45, Fig. 5-2), ch. 19 (pp. 145–146); Photon paper §1, §5

Electron

E1 Spin opened on alternating bands, which Fleming does not describe done

Fleming
Quantum dipoles tend to rotate in the same direction while being polarized. Fig. 2 shows every dipole round the charge curling the same way. The two counter-moving layers of §4 are the two ends of each dipole, not successive bands.
Laboratory before
The default reversed the turn of each complete band; the shared preference was the alternative. The notes already label alternation an extension.

Fixed. Shared preference is the default. Alternating bands remains under Adjacent shells › Alternate and as a saved view in Help, labelled as an extension Fleming does not describe. Saved files that chose alternation keep it.

Electron paper §3 (Fig. 2), §4

E2 Every pair snapped fully into line at every distance done

Fleming
A small amount of charge only causes a small percentage of zeptons to orient … and most are only oriented to a slight degree. The same total amount of polarization occurs at each distance away from the charge; it is just spread out over increasingly larger volumes. Pairs are continually being produced in somewhat random orientations.
Laboratory before
All 2,197 sample pairs ended exactly radial, near and far alike, and every replacement was born already aligned. The inverse-square thinning lived only in the separate analytic arrows.

Fixed (model electron-polarization/4). Each generation is born at its own random angle and turns toward the electron during its life: all the way inside 1.6 R, and beyond it by a share chosen so the mean alignment falls with the square of distance. Near pairs line up; far pairs stay mostly random with a slight lean. Polarization no longer comes to rest: every replacement turns again, which is the continual process Fleming gives as the origin of spin. A pair is drawn as bright as it is aligned.

New readout. Dock › Polarization sums the alignment of the displayed pairs shell by shell. Farther shells hold more pairs, each turned less, and the total stays level: the same polarization through every sphere, read from the dipoles themselves and not from a reference formula. A test checks the displayed pairs against the rule.

Scale. The 1.6 R radius is a magnification. Fleming says the lean is slight and gives no figure; drawn to scale it would be invisible. Faraday lines now trace the alignment the pairs share (their mean), since any single generation is random.

Book ch. 6 (pp. 49–53); Electron paper §2–3

E3 Mass from excluded energy was listed as blocked, but the book gives the recipe done

Fleming
Chapter 16 gives the excluded energy density between two shell diameters and worked numbers: electron 6.833×10²⁵ GeV/cm³ at a Compton-wavelength diameter, inner 2.123 pm, outer 2.472 pm; proton 3.315×10³⁸ GeV/cm³, inner 1.586 fm, outer 1.924 fm.
Laboratory before
The video notes list the mass budget as waiting on a paper that is not in the repository. I checked: ρ = ħ(ω₂⁴ − ω₁⁴)/(8π²c³) with ω = 2πc/d reproduces the book's 1.124×10²⁶ GeV/cm³ exactly.

The drawing follows Fig. 16-1: short fluctuations inside the shell, long ones outside, and the band in between excluded.

Extended Casimir

C1 Attraction did not show the chain shortening done

Fleming
When each successive zepton vanishes, the space available for the next pair is smaller, so smaller wavelength zeptons take its place … Over time, the length of the chain of zeptons is shortened.
Laboratory before
All zeptons opened to one fixed width whatever their lifetime, and replacements in the bridge were the same size as their predecessors.

Fixed. A zepton now opens in proportion to its lifetime, so its drawn width is its wavelength. Between an electron and a proton, the replacement of an aligned zepton in the bridge is shorter-lived and smaller (by a display factor of 0.72), and smaller again as released charges close in, while the field outside keeps its full range. A replacement there is also never longer-lived than the zepton it replaces, so the chain at each place can only shorten; it settles at the shortest the space allows. Between like charges nothing shortens, and each gap-filling birth is smaller than every ordinary zepton. The loupe reports each zepton's wavelength. Pressure signs and the motion they drive are unchanged and still tested.

Book ch. 6 (p. 55, Fig. 6-12); Casimir paper §4

Van der Waals and plates

V1 The plate picture used standing waves where Fleming draws excluded zeptons done

Fleming
Fig. 3-3: ovals of every size outside the plates, only small ones between. Vacuum fluctuations with wavelengths that are longer than the distance between the plates will be excluded from that space.
Laboratory before
A uniform pattern of identical ovals outside, and three sine curves labelled n = 1, 2, 3 between the plates.

Fixed. The plate stage now draws fourteen sample wavelengths (30 to 1000 nm) as ovals of matching length, all of them outside the plates and only those shorter than the gap between them. Move the gap and the count in the caption changes: 4 of 14 fit at 100 nm, 13 of 14 at 1000 nm. The standing-wave curves remain as an optional layer, off by default. The pressure, force and energy readouts are unchanged.

Book ch. 3 (pp. 29–30, Figs 3-3, 3-4)

What already matches Fleming

  • Zepton energy and lifetime. The medium assigns E = hf/2 and lifetime 1/f, with an energy ledger that balances.
  • Birth, separation, collapse. Pairs appear at a point, open, and close on a fixed centre in every lab.
  • Positive ends toward the electron, and Faraday lines traced from the pair orientations themselves.
  • Nothing orbits. Spin is shown as local turns at fixed centres with replacement generations, as §3 and §6 require.
  • Both charge ends counter-move in the charge-motion close-up, the basis of Fleming's g ≈ 2.
  • Photon as a chain. One induced pair per half-wavelength, each a half-turn, alternating sense, half the energy in the pair and half in the field.
  • Like and unlike charges. Deflection and gap-filling between electrons, collapsing aligned neighbours between electron and proton, motion from the pressure difference only.
  • Tracking a moving charge. Pairs above and below the path turn in opposite senses as the electron passes (Fig. 6-14).

Suggested next steps

In rough order of how directly Fleming's sources support them.

  • Absorption in the Light lab. End the run at an absorber and show the field's energy collapsing into it with the central pair, the event Fleming uses to argue that fields outrun light (Photon paper §1, §6, Fig. 1).
  • The proton as a second polarizer. Reverse the orientation of the surrounding pairs, shrink the shell to the charge radius, and show the polarization rate 1,386 times the electron's (Radii paper §2–4). The mass study already handles the proton.
  • Double slit. Chapter 19 gives a concrete picture: the central pair goes straight while its field passes both slits (Fig. 19-3). It is on the planned list and now has a field model to build on.
  • Magnetic attraction and repulsion in the Extended Casimir lab, which §4 describes with the same two mechanisms as the electric cases.
  • Zero-point spectrum in the medium. Sample frequencies with the density Fleming uses for energy (more short wavelengths than long), instead of evenly across a band.

Open questions and observations

  • One set of printed numbers in chapter 16 does not follow from its own recipe (the electron shell about the Compton wavelength; see E3). Everything else in the chapter reproduces.
  • Sign conventions in the photon figures disagree. Book Fig. 4-9 and 5-1 draw the electric arrow toward the positive end of the pair; Fig. 3 of the photon paper draws it away. The caption of Fig. 4-9 says the magnetic field points into the page while its symbol is the usual one for out. I follow the book's stated rule (field toward the positive end, magnetic field along the rotation axis by the right-hand grip) and keep electric × magnetic along the direction of travel.
  • How far an idle zepton turns is not stated. Fleming gives 180° as the limit and as what a photon's pair does. For the undisturbed medium I use the half-turn for every pair, the virtual half-wavelength photon of chapter 4.
  • Not yet attempted (each needs a law Fleming leaves open): the van der Waals torque that sets the polarization rate, the inverse-square pressure law, the 782 keV exchange events, tired light. The proton as a second polarizer, and the faster-than-light field response on absorption, are feasible next steps.
