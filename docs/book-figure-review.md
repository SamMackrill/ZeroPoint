The yardstick is Fleming’s own text and figures ([fidelity review](fleming-fidelity-report.html)).

Status: **to review**, **agreed** (no change), **changed** (fixed and agreed), **held** (open question).

## Notes by figure

### Fig. 3-1 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** the system is dynamic. The dipoles contract or expand and are also attracted or repelled, so animate it rather than use arrows for the motion.
- **Agreed:** the green field arrows (Debye’s 𝔈) stay. They map the force on each dipole’s charges, not the motion itself.
- **Done:** the dipoles are released at rest from the starting distance. In I they move apart while their charges draw in; in II they move together while their charges spread, until they touch. Then the motion repeats. Debye’s small arrows and the force arrows fade while the figure plays (the #73 rule). The field arrows shorten as the field weakens. The card’s notes say that the motion and its timings are ours.
- **Owner:** in II the dipoles should not touch. Fleming’s chains are only *virtually* in contact (p. 52), and Debye’s figure is of classical ion dipoles, which illustrate the idea but are not zeptons.
- **Done:** II now stops with the facing charges 0.6 dipole lengths apart, centre to centre. The card’s notes give the reason and say that the distance is a display choice.
- **Owner:** the label “Green: the field of the other dipole, at each dipole” was clumsy. Debye simply letters the arrow.
- **Done:** each field arrow is now labelled *E*, as elsewhere in the companion. The notes say that Debye’s 𝔈 is the old Fraktur letter for the electric field. (It is a Fraktur letter, not a Greek one.)
- **Owner:** more strictly, a field is just a mapping, an indicator of force. **Agreed:** this is Fleming’s view (p. 52). The comment and the notes no longer call E the cause of the motion. The notes now say that E maps the force a charge would feel, and quote p. 52.

### Fig. 3-2 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** for consistency, use E instead of “local electric field”.
- **Done:** the label above the arrow is now *E*. The controls read “Strength of E” and “E points to the right”. Fleming’s caption keeps his own wording.
- **Owner:** A and B are the same hydrogen atom, without and with an electric field; the text should say so. **Agreed:** Fleming’s text on p. 29 follows one atom, although his caption calls B “the second hydrogen atom”.
- **Done:** the card’s text says that A and B are one atom and quotes the fuller p. 29 passage. The stage labels read “A · no E” and “B · in E”, and the Try steps refer to A and B as one atom. A note records that the caption speaks of a second atom while the text and the diagram follow one.

### Fig. 3-3 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** as the plates close, the outside fluctuations should fill the space outside. At the moment a blank area opens up, which is not what happens. There should also be some sign of the force pressing the plates together: an extension of the original, but important.
- **Done (first pass, superseded by the next entry):** the outside fluctuations are now scattered across the whole stage. Every one whose centre lies outside the plates is drawn, clipped at the plates’ outer faces, so the vacuum beside the plates stays at the same density whatever the gap. A block arrow presses on each plate’s outer face. The arrows grow with the push, which goes as 1/d⁴ (Equation 3-1, p. 31; p. 30 for the pressure from outside). A readout gives the push relative to the widest gap. The notes say that the arrows are not in Fleming’s figure and that their length is a log display scale.
- **Owner:** the fluctuations should not clip into the plates.
- **Done:** no clipping now. Every fluctuation is drawn whole, and one that would reach into a plate at its fullest is not drawn. Outside the plates, a fluctuation is drawn only if all of it clears the outer faces. Between them, each one leans upright as the gap narrows, stands upright if it would not otherwise fit, and is placed so that it clears both plates.
- **Owner:** “The force between the two plates is retarded” does not seem right; would pressure be better? **Discussed:** that sentence is Fleming’s caption, which stays as printed. “Retarded” is Casimir and Polder’s word for the force, and Fleming reads it as “retarded, or reduced” (p. 29). **Done:** a paragraph in the card’s text explains this, quotes p. 30 to show that what drops is the pressure between the plates, and says that the force is the difference.
