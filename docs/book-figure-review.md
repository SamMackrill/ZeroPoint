# Review of the book companion figures

A figure-by-figure review of the companion (`book/index.html`) with the owner, begun 2026-11-18. Each figure is checked against Fleming’s printed figure and the text around it (*The Zero-Point Universe*, `docs/papers/`), viewed live, discussed and fixed. The yardstick is Fleming’s own text and figures ([fidelity review](fleming-fidelity-report.html)).

Status: **to review**, **agreed** (no change), **changed** (fixed and agreed), **held** (open question).

## Notes by figure

### Fig. 3-1 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** the system is dynamic. The dipoles contract or expand and are also attracted or repelled, so animate it rather than use arrows for the motion.
- **Agreed:** the green field arrows (Debye’s 𝔈) stay. They map the force on each dipole’s charges, not the motion itself.
- **Done:** the dipoles are released at rest from the starting distance. In I they move apart while their charges draw in; in II they move together while their charges spread, until they touch. Then the motion repeats. Debye’s small arrows and the force arrows fade while the figure plays (the #73 rule). The field arrows shorten as the field weakens. The card’s notes say that the motion and its timings are ours.
- **Owner:** in II the dipoles should not touch. Fleming’s chains are only *virtually* in contact (p. 52), and Debye’s figure is of classical ion dipoles, which illustrate the idea but are not zeptons.
- **Done:** II now stops with the facing charges 0.6 dipole lengths apart, centre to centre. The card’s notes give the reason and say that the distance is a display choice.
- **Owner:** the label “Green: the field of the other dipole, at each dipole” was clumsy. Debye simply letters the arrow.
- **Done:** each field arrow is now labelled *E*, as elsewhere in the companion. The notes say that Debye’s 𝔈 is the old Fraktur letter for the electric field. (It is a Fraktur letter, not a Greek one.)
- **Owner:** more strictly, a field is just a mapping, an indicator of force. **Agreed:** this is Fleming’s view (p. 52). The comment and the notes no longer call E the cause of the motion. The notes now say that E maps the force a charge would feel, and quote p. 52.

### Fig. 3-2 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** for consistency, use E instead of “local electric field”.
- **Done:** the label above the arrow is now *E*. The controls read “Strength of E” and “E points to the right”. Fleming’s caption keeps his own wording.
- **Owner:** A and B are the same hydrogen atom, without and with an electric field; the text should say so. **Agreed:** Fleming’s text on p. 29 follows one atom, although his caption calls B “the second hydrogen atom”.
- **Done:** the card’s text says that A and B are one atom and quotes the fuller p. 29 passage. The stage labels read “A · no E” and “B · in E”, and the Try steps refer to A and B as one atom. A note records that the caption speaks of a second atom while the text and the diagram follow one.

### Fig. 3-3 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** as the plates close, the outside fluctuations should fill the space outside. At the moment a blank area opens up, which is not what happens. There should also be some sign of the force pressing the plates together: an extension of the original, but important.
- **Done (first pass, superseded by the next entry):** the outside fluctuations are now scattered across the whole stage. Every one whose centre lies outside the plates is drawn, clipped at the plates’ outer faces, so the vacuum beside the plates stays at the same density whatever the gap. A block arrow presses on each plate’s outer face. The arrows grow with the push, which goes as 1/d⁴ (Equation 3-1, p. 31; p. 30 for the pressure from outside). A readout gives the push relative to the widest gap. The notes say that the arrows are not in Fleming’s figure and that their length is a log display scale.
- **Owner:** the fluctuations should not clip into the plates.
- **Done:** no clipping now. Every fluctuation is drawn whole, and one that would reach into a plate at its fullest is not drawn. Outside the plates, a fluctuation is drawn only if all of it clears the outer faces. Between them, each one leans upright as the gap narrows, stands upright if it would not otherwise fit, and is placed so that it clears both plates.
- **Owner:** “The force between the two plates is retarded” does not seem right; would pressure be better? **Discussed:** that sentence is Fleming’s caption, which stays as printed. “Retarded” is Casimir and Polder’s word for the force, and Fleming reads it as “retarded, or reduced” (p. 29). **Done:** a paragraph in the card’s text explains this, quotes p. 30 to show that what drops is the pressure between the plates, and says that the force is the difference.

### Fig. 3-4 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** the area slider adds nothing here.
- **Done:** the slider is gone. The force readout is now “Force on 1 cm² of plate”, and the notes say that the force is in proportion to the area. The Try step about the area is gone.
- **Owner:** the text on the diagram clips, and it adds little. **Done:** the three labels are gone, as in Fleming’s figure, which has none. The caption and the magnify toggle carry the meaning. Only the *d =* dimension remains.
- **Owner:** the slider’s label jumps between one line and two as the value’s text changes length. **Done, for the whole companion:** a slider’s value now stays on the label’s row, right-aligned in room reserved for its longest reading, and only the label wraps. Ten sliders printed the current reading instead of the value they were given; they now use that value, so the longest reading can be measured. An automated sweep of all 91 sliders finds none that moves, and two kit tests guard against both faults.
- **Owner:** the single “d” wrapping onto a line of its own is unnecessary. **Done:** the label is now “Gap between the plates, d”, which fits on one line. For the whole companion: a reading longer than 9 characters always sits on its own line under the label, and a short one sits beside it. A label that must wrap does so evenly (`text-wrap: balance`). Every label now takes one or two lines (before this, the room reserved for long readings squeezed some to five), and a sweep of all 91 sliders still finds none that jumps.
- **Owner:** the readouts’ numbers wrap in the same way (“1.3 × 10⁻⁴ / Pa”), and the label rewraps as the value changes. **Done, for the whole companion:** a readout’s label now sits above its value, so the value has the full width and can never rewrap the label. Labels that wrap do so evenly. A sweep of all 195 readouts at their starting settings finds no numeric value that wraps. Two values that are phrases do wrap, in 4-9 (“0 of its peak, into the page”) and 26-1. To raise when we reach them: 4-9’s phrase changes as the animation runs.

### Fig. 4-1 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** the Antimatter/Holes switch only changes the text and adds little, unless holes can be shown. **Discussed:** Fleming gives no picture of a hole: “we do not have a reasonable model for a hole in space” (p. 34). His caption reads the zero line as the division between matter and antimatter. **Agreed:** remove the switch.
- **Done:** the switch is gone, and the labels read matter above the line and antimatter below. The card’s text quotes Fleming’s reason in full and says that the diagram follows the antimatter reading. The Try step about the switch is gone.

### Fig. 4-2 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** the text explicitly mentions rotation, but none of the dipoles rotate. **Agreed:** they rotated only while the reader dragged the charge. Fleming says what the rotation is on p. 56: “as a free electron moves, the zepton dipoles rotate to maintain their orientation relative to the stable charge”.
- **Done:** once a charge is added, it now moves back and forth across the sea by itself (“The charge moves”, on by default), easing round at each end, and the dipoles near its path rotate to keep pointing at it. Turning the motion off leaves the charge where it is and lets the reader drag it. The card quotes p. 56, the Try steps and remark describe the motion, and the notes call the path and speed display choices.
- **Owner:** unsure about the free charge; the dipoles should rotate naturally through mutual attraction and repulsion. Is the free charge in the book? **Answer:** no, the printed figure is only the sea; the charge illustrated the p. 34 sentence. Fleming’s pairs rotate (a half-turn per half-wavelength, p. 39), and around a photon they rotate in response to the central pair (p. 44). I found no statement that mutual attraction is what turns the pairs of the sea. **Chosen:** pairs rotate, no charge.
- **Done:** the free charge, its controls and its motion are gone. Each pair forms at a random angle, turns half a turn through its life (p. 39), one way or the other at random, and is gone. The card quotes p. 39, and the notes point to chapter 6 for orientation to a charge.

### Fig. 4-9 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** this tries to show a 3D interaction on a flat page; it would be better in 3D, so that the reader can turn it and see E and B. The E and B numbers flicker and keep wrapping. The charges change magically as the pair rotates, which cannot be intended.
- **Cause of the charge “swap”:** the half-turn looped straight back to 0°, so the + end jumped from right to left. Fleming’s pair lives only for its half-turn (p. 39).
- **Done:** the figure is now drawn in three dimensions with three.js, loaded only when needed. The reader can drag to turn the view and choose Turned, As printed or Along path. The pair turns in its plane, E is beside it in that plane, B runs along the axis, and a dashed line marks the photon’s path. Each pair fades out at the end of its half-turn, and after a moment a new pair fades in at 0°, so nothing jumps. The rotation arrow shows only when the motion is paused (the #73 rule). The E trace through the half-turn sits underneath. The readouts are whole percentages padded to a fixed width, and their direction moved into the label. Where WebGL is not available, the flat page view is drawn instead. The notes say that the 3D view and the fading are ours. Two new tests cover this.
- **Owner:** what is the cut-off “path” label? **Answer:** it labelled the dashed line, the photon’s path, and its box was too narrow. **Done:** it now reads “photon’s path” in full, and an arrowhead on the line shows the photon moving along x (Fig. 5-2, p. 44).

### Fig. 5-1 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** the pair-spacing slider adds little to the illustration. **Done:** the slider is gone, the spacing is fixed at 68 units, and its Try step is removed. The E and B readouts take the 4-9 treatment: whole percentages at a fixed width, with the direction in the label. The shared formatter (`percentOfPeak`) now lives in the kit.
- **Owner:** the text by each pair is hard to read; it should fade in and out more slowly. The annihilation text is too long (“annihilate & reverse”?) and hard to see. **Done:** “pair N” fades in as the pair forms and out as its turn ends. “annihilate & reverse”, the owner’s wording and Fleming’s sequence on p. 44, fades in as the pair ends and lingers about a second into the next pair. Each label has its own line and is a size larger, so the two never overlap. The stage is 22 units taller to make room.

### Fig. 5-2 (agreed 2026-11-18, no change)

- **Owner:** accurate to the original, but hard to understand. After the explanation (the central pair is the particle; the pairs turning in sympathy round it are the wave, p. 44; in reality only slightly biased, p. 45), the owner judged the card fine as it is: the Light laboratory, which the card links to, illustrates this better.

### Fig. 6-1 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** this is a bit flickery; make it smoother. **Cause:** each atom’s electron jumped to a fresh random place 14 times a second. **Done:** each electron now moves smoothly round its proton. It is slowest on the far side from the free electron and quickest on the near side, so it spends most of its time away and the proton faces the free electron (p. 49). With no free electron it circles evenly. The dashed shell is centred on the electron’s average place.
- **Owner:** better, but the polarization of the hydrogen is still hard to see. **Done:** the electron now dwells much more strongly on the far side (an eccentric-anomaly orbit, smooth and always moving forward), and the polarization reaches further. A mark-coloured line runs from each proton to its electron’s average place, showing each atom’s dipole. The atoms are drawn half as large again. A test checks that the motion is smooth and forward-only, and that a lone atom is neutral on average.

### Fig. 6-2 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** the slider (the number of lines of zeptons) adds nothing and makes the figure more confusing. **Done:** the slider is gone. The figure draws eight lines, as printed, and the Try step no longer mentions it.

### Fig. 6-4 (changed, agreed 2026-11-18)

- **Owner:** would this be clearer in 3D, since it is a sphere? **My view:** yes, and the flat drawing also misleads. On a page, the lines cross circles, whose length grows as r, so the picture alone suggests 1/r. The inverse square comes from the sphere’s area, 4πr², as Fleming says. **Chosen:** build it in 3D.
- **Done:** the 3D view (three.js, loaded on demand) has 26 lines coming in evenly from every direction, a translucent sphere with three faint great circles, a dot where each line crosses it, and a highlighted patch of fixed area. As the sphere grows, the patch holds fewer crossings. The reader can drag to orbit, and the wheel scrolls the page. The density curve sits underneath. The flat page view, with eight lines, is the fallback. The shared 3D helpers moved to `three/common.ts` and the error boundary to the kit (`FallbackOnError`). The notes give the reason for 3D and say that the line count and the patch are display choices.
